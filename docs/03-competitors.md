## C. National chains
- **Starbucks, Dunkin' and others** have many locations across Miami-Dade, including towers in Brickell. They win on convenience, apps and loyalty programs. ([Yelp listing](https://m.yelp.com/search?find_desc=Starbucks&find_loc=Miami%2C+FL)) *To verify: the exact number of locations.*
- **Delivery apps** (DoorDash, Uber Eats) let competitors reach customers without being on a walkable street.

## How the market is laid out
- **Wynwood, Brickell and Edgewater** are the most crowded with specialty shops. Brickell alone has at least 5 specialty shops plus Cuban counters and chains. ([BrewAtlas Brickell](https://brewatlas.co/miami/brickell))
- **Coral Gables** combines specialty, coworking and ventanita formats.
- **Gaps to look into:** Little River, Little Haiti, Allapattah, the MiMo/Upper Eastside, Doral, Kendall and South Miami-Dade appear to have fewer specialty shops. *Assumption:* this needs checking on the ground.
- **What competitors do to stand out:** roast their own beans, add coworking, sell functional drinks, tie in art, sell food (pastries or pizza), and blend Cuban and specialty coffee.

## What it means for us
- If we don't roast, it will be hard to compete head-on with Panther or Vice City in Wynwood or Brickell. The concept needs a clear angle. Options include a "Cuban × specialty" menu, a mixed ventanita and café format, a strong food program, or an underserved neighborhood.
- Offer a well-made, fairly priced Cuban espresso line ($3–4). It keeps local regulars coming in every day.
