# 8. Biggest Risks

Ranked by **likelihood × impact**. The ranking is our own judgment and is meant to be challenged.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Evidence | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Undercapitalization: running out of cash before break-even** | High | Severe | Owners underestimate costs by 20–50% ([NuMarket](https://numarket.co/blog/true-cost-of-starting-a-coffee-shop-and-how-to-fund-it)); profitability takes 12–24 months ([Delta Capital](https://deltacapitalgroup.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-coffee-shop/)) | Hold 6 months of working capital plus a 10–15% contingency; SBA 7(a) or microloan; phase the menu and decor |
| 2 | **Thin margins / volume below break-even** | High | Severe | Average net margin 2.5–7% ([VantaInsights](https://vantainsights.com/insights/coffee-shop-profit-margins)); 42% of restaurant operators were not profitable in the prior year (NRA 2026, via [DoorDash](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-coffee-shop)) | Choose the site by measured foot traffic; target 250+ transactions a day; raise the average ticket with food and retail |
| 3 | **Lease and occupancy cost** | High | Severe | Miami retail asking rent ~$45/sq ft plus NNN; vacancy around 2.7% leaves tenants little bargaining power ([MMG](https://mmgequitypartners.com/south-florida-retail-summary/)) | Keep occupancy at or below 10–12% of revenue; take a former café; get free rent and a TI allowance, make the lease contingent on permits, sign the shortest term possible with renewal options; avoid an unlimited personal guarantee |
| 4 | **Rising labor costs and staff turnover** | High | High | Minimum wage $15 from Sept 2026, expected to rise with inflation after that; labor is 28–35% of revenue ([HGA](https://hospitalitygrowthadvisors.com/insights/restaurant-labor-cost-percentage)) | Schedule against POS sales data; design the bar so fewer staff can handle the peak; train staff well; keep opening hours lean |
| 5 | **Hurricanes, flooding and insurance cost** | Medium (each year) | Severe | Season runs June 1–Nov 30. Florida businesses pay **15–40% more** for insurance ([Florida PlanFinder](https://floridaplanfinder.com/small-business/florida-small-business-insurance-cost-by-industry/)); some South Florida property policies run **$18K–$75K+ a year** ([Greene Insurance](https://www.greeneinsurance.com/cost/commercial-property-insurance-florida)); hurricane deductibles are a percentage of value ([Atesa](https://atesariskadvisors.com/blog/florida-restaurant-insurance-guide-2026)) | Avoid flood-zone ground floors where possible; get business interruption, flood and equipment-breakdown cover; keep a hurricane plan (shutters, generator or backup ice, cloud POS); hold cash for the deductible |
| 6 | **Seasonality and summer slump** | High | Medium | Tourist peak Nov–Apr; heat and rain in summer | Choose a site with mostly residents and office workers; build B2B and wholesale; launch iced drinks and loyalty pushes in summer; adjust staffing by season |
| 7 | **Competition and price anchoring** | High | Medium | Dense specialty supply in Wynwood, Brickell and Edgewater; ventanitas sell a colada for ~$3 (see 03-competitors) | Stand out clearly; offer a fairly priced Cuban espresso line; consider underserved neighborhoods |
| 8 | **Swings in green coffee prices and tariffs** | Medium | Medium | Arabica peaked at $4.41/lb in Feb 2025, was ~$2.72 in Nov 2026; tariffs on Brazil came and went in 2025 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/coffee), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/global-coffee-prices-plunge-after-trump-removes-tariffs-brazil-2025-11-21/)) | Sign 6–12-month fixed-price contracts with roasters; keep beverage COGS at 15–20% through pricing; use a menu-pricing process that can change prices quickly |
| 9 | **Permit and buildout delays** | Medium | Medium | Rent paid while closed during delays: $2K–$10K; buildout overruns 10–30% ([Bellwether](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/coffee-shop-startup-costs)) | Use an expeditor or architect who knows the city's process; start rent only once the CU is issued; take a second-generation food space |
| 10 | **Equipment failure** | Medium | Medium | Without the espresso machine, there are no sales | Service contract; backup grinder; batch brew as a fallback; a filter and preventive maintenance plan (Miami's water is hard) |
| 11 | **Compliance missteps** (food safety, E-Verify, tip pools, sales tax) | Low–Medium | Medium | See 05 and 06 | Compliance checklist and calendar; payroll provider; a certified food manager on every shift |

## Early warning indicators for the agent team to watch
- Weekly transactions below plan for 4 weeks in a row
- Labor above 35% or COGS above 32% for a full month
- Cash runway under 3 months
- Google rating below 4.5
- An active NOAA tropical system with Miami in its forecast cone (start the hurricane plan)
