# Bilingual localization (Miami)

## Principles
- Write natural Miami English and Spanish; do not translate word for word. Spanglish is fine where it sounds local.
- Lead with the language of the audience of that channel; menus and signs show both.
- Keep Cuban coffee terms in Spanish everywhere: cafecito, colada, cortadito, café con leche, ventanita.

## Glossary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cafecito / café cubano | Small, sweetened Cuban espresso (sugar whipped in, espumita) |
| Colada | Larger serving of cafecito with small cups for sharing |
| Cortadito | Cuban espresso with steamed milk |
| Café con leche | Espresso with lots of hot milk, often with tostada |
| Ventanita | Walk-up window serving coffee and snacks |

## Review checklist
- Correct accents and spelling; consistent tone (tú vs. usted chosen on purpose; tú is common for casual brands).
- No terms that read as mocking or stereotyping Cuban or Latin American culture.
- Prices, sizes and allergens identical in both languages.
- Check names for unintended meanings across Cuban, Venezuelan, Colombian, Puerto Rican and other Spanish varieties common in Miami.
