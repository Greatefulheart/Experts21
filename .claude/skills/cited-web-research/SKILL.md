# Cited web research

## Procedure
1. Check `docs/` first; only search for what is missing or may be stale.
2. Search with specific terms (place, year, agency). Run independent searches in parallel.
3. Open the most authoritative result rather than trusting a snippet.
4. Record each fact with: the claim, the number, the source URL, the publisher, and the date (of the data, not only of the page).

## Source ranking (best first)
1. Government and regulators: floridajobs.org, myfloridalicense.com (DBPR), floridarevenue.com, miami.gov, miamidade.gov, census.gov, bls.gov, dol.gov, fema.gov, noaa.gov.
2. Official industry bodies and data: SCA, NCA, GMCVB, ICE futures data.
3. Reputable press and research firms (Reuters, IBISWorld summaries, Perfect Daily Grind).
4. Vendors and directories (equipment sellers, lenders, BrewAtlas, Yelp). Note their commercial interest.
5. Forums (Reddit). Use only as signals, never as the only source for a number.

## Rules
- Never invent a source, URL, figure or date. If you cannot find it, say so and add a "To verify" item.
- Flag data older than 2 years, figures quoted second-hand, and vendor claims.
- Convert units and years consistently (per sq ft per year, 2026 dollars) and say when you did.
- Keep personal or client details out of search queries.
