# 5. Permits and Licenses

> **Jurisdiction matters.** Miami-Dade County has more than 30 municipalities. The steps below follow the **City of Miami** path. Miami Beach, Coral Gables and other cities, and unincorporated Miami-Dade, have their own portals and rules. This is research, not legal advice. Confirm everything with each agency.

## Checklist

### Business setup (state and federal)
| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Form an LLC or corporation and register any trade name | Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) | Register the trade name if it differs from the legal name |
| EIN | IRS | Needed for payroll and bank accounts |
| Sales and use tax registration | Florida Department of Revenue | Collect **7%** in Miami-Dade (6% state plus 1% county surtax); file returns on form DR-15. ([FDOR](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/sales_tax.aspx)) |
| Reemployment (unemployment) tax account | Florida Department of Revenue | Required once you have employees. This account also covers the E-Verify certification (see 06-staffing). |

### Food service
| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Food service license (seating or non-seating)** | Florida DBPR, Division of Hotels & Restaurants | Coffee shops are specifically named as food service establishments. Submit the license and plan review applications together where plan review is required. ([DBPR guide](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/foodservice-guide/)) |
| DBPR fees | DBPR | Seating license for **1–49 seats: $262**; 50–149 seats: $273. The fee page shows plan review at $0. ([DBPR fees](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/food-fees/)) *To verify: current and prorated fees.* |
| **Certified Food Protection Manager** | Accredited exam provider; DBPR enforces | Managers must be certified **within 30 days of being hired**; recertify every 5 years. ([DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/food-lodging/food-manager/)) |
| Food employee training | DBPR-approved provider | All food employees need approved training. *To verify: the deadline, which is commonly cited as within 60 days of hire.* |
| Retail food permit (only if selling packaged retail food) | FDACS | Applies to retail food establishments; check whether selling bagged beans or packaged goods triggers it. ([FDACS](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Food/Food-Establishments/Retail-Food-Establishment-Permit)) |

### Local (City of Miami and Miami-Dade County)
| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Zoning check** | City of Miami (Miami 21 zoning code) | Confirm the zoning allows food service *before* signing a lease |
| **Certificate of Use (CU)** | City of Miami | Required for any new or relocated business. **The CU application includes the Fire Safety Permit.** Food service establishments need DBPR approval as part of the process. ([City of Miami CU](https://www.miami.gov/Business-Licenses/Business-Licensing/Get-a-Certificate-of-Use-CU)) |
| **Business Tax Receipt (city)** | City of Miami | Applied for through the same portal as the CU |
| **Local Business Tax Receipt (county)** | Miami-Dade Tax Collector | Requires a sworn statement that the business does not trade with Cuba in violation of federal law (FS 205.0532). Allow up to 3 business days. ([MDC Tax Collector](https://mdctaxcollector.gov/services/local-business-tax-receipt)) |
| **Building permits** (buildout, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, signs) | City or county building department | Required for construction. Permit delays are a common cause of paying rent while closed. |
| **Environmental (DERM) operating permit and water/sewer (WASD) review** | Miami-Dade RER / DERM, WASD | Some uses need a DERM permit *before* the CU. Grease-trap and interceptor requirements apply if there is cooking. ([Miami-Dade EPS](https://www.miamidade.gov/Apps/RER/EPSPortal/PlanReview/CUApplications/Landing)) |
| **Sidewalk café permit** (outdoor seating on a public sidewalk) | City of Miami, Department of Resilience & Public Works | ([City of Miami](https://www.miami.gov/Permits-Construction/Permit-Catalog/Sidewalk-Cafe-Permit?oc_lang=en-US)) |
| Sign permit | City building or zoning department | |

### Other
- **Music licenses** (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC) if music is played in the shop: about $500–$1,500 a year in total. ([VantaInsights](https://vantainsights.com/insights/coffee-shop-startup-costs))
- **Liquor license**, only if serving beer, wine or coffee cocktails: DBPR Division of Alcoholic Beverages & Tobacco. This is out of scope for now.
- **Required workplace posters**, including the Florida minimum wage poster and federal posters.

## Suggested order
1. Choose the concept, then confirm zoning and the likely permit path for 2–3 candidate sites.
2. Form the entity, get an EIN, open a bank account.
3. Negotiate the lease. Make it **contingent on permits being issued** and include free rent during buildout.
4. Get architect drawings, then building permits and DBPR plan review (together with the license application).
5. Do the buildout, then inspections (building, fire, DBPR).
6. Get the CU, the city BTR and the county LBTR.
7. Register for sales tax and reemployment tax; enroll in E-Verify if needed.
8. Certify a food manager, train staff, do a soft opening.

**Timeline (Estimate):** 3–9 months from signing the lease to opening. A bare-shell space is at the slow end. ([Bellwether](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/coffee-shop-startup-costs) puts buildout alone at 16–24 weeks for a shell.)
